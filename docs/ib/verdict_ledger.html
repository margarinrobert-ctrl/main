Machine learning

Capacity is monotonically harmful

141 causal features at each breakout, labelled with the R the trade actually earned. Purged and embargoed walk-forward — overlapping trades make a naive K-fold train on the answer. Out-of-fold AUC, higher is better.

The shuffled twin earned its keep immediately. Run the deepest network on randomly permuted labels and its top-decile P&L came out at +0.2633 R — more than any model managed on real labels. That column is noise, and only the shuffled control reveals it.
Why a good classifier still loses

The models buy win rate by selling the tail

MarketModelWin% allWin% picked R allR pickedp90 R allp90 R picked
Both markets show the model doing exactly what it was trained to do — win rate rises from 30–33% to 36–39%. The difference is the tail. On NQ the 90th-percentile R falls 1.740 → 1.340 and the strategy goes negative; on US30 the tail holds 1.872 → 1.896 and the same win-rate gain drops to the bottom line. Train on win/lose and you get a win-rate optimiser. A breakout system earns in the tail.

Four families — ATR percentile, ATR expansion, ATR against its own average, ATR as a fraction of price — every parameter in a declared grid, each against a random filter of the same selectivity. Share of cells clearing that control at p ≤ 0.05, where chance is 5%.

Both survivors are the same condition — trade only when ATR sits in the bottom fifth of its own last 500 bars. It clears on both blocks on US30 and does nothing on NQ, where only 4% of 120 cells beat their control against a 5% chance rate. Two survivors out of 240 tested is roughly what chance delivers at p ≤ 0.05; the pair is worth watching, not trading.
