How it works

1. Measure the market's memory. Over a rolling window it runs rescaled-range (R/S) analysis to estimate the Hurst exponent H. H>0.55 = trending (moves persist), H<0.45 = mean-reverting (moves reverse), ~0.5 = random walk.

2. Pick the matching tool. In trends it uses FRAMA (Ehlers' Fractal Adaptive Moving Average) — long while price is above it. In reverting regimes it fades extremes using a z-score of price vs its recent mean. In random regimes it stays flat.

3. Backtest honestly. Signals are computed on day t and the position is taken on t+1 (no peeking ahead), then compared to simply holding the index.

⚠ Read before trusting it

This is an educational tool, not investment advice. The Hurst exponent is biased in small samples and an H≠0.5 is statistically compatible with a pure random walk — it does not prove a real edge.

A backtest is a description of the past with full hindsight on parameters. It ignores commissions, slippage, financing and taxes, and says little about the future.

Treat regime/Hurst as one input among many, not a buy button. Size small, paper-trade first.
