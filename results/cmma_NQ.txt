2. WHAT THE NOTEBOOK REPORTS, AND WHY IT IS NOT A SHARPE RATIO
========================================================================================================
  notebook:  eq = pnl.cumsum();  sr = eq.mean() / eq.std()   ->   0.875
  That is the mean of an EQUITY CURVE divided by its own standard deviation. It is
  large for any curve that trends and it has no sampling interpretation: it does not
  scale with the square root of time, it is not comparable between strategies, and it
  cannot be given a standard error. The same series scored as a return stream:
  annualised Sharpe on the daily return series: +0.83 +- 0.58
  The two happen to be close here (0.875 against 0.83), which is a coincidence of this sample and not a validation of the
  formula. And the standard error is the point: +-0.58 on a 3-year sample means this
  Sharpe is not distinguishable from zero OR from 1.5 without more data.
