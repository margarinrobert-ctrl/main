THE IB-25 RETRACEMENT FOR ES -- what can be answered without an ES feed, and what cannot.

NO ES SERIES EXISTS ON THIS BRANCH. `research/datasets.py` lists sixteen feeds and none is the
S&P; CLAUDE.md has carried the line "ES has never been supplied" since STUDY_TURTLE_15M. So an ES
backtest cannot be produced here and none is claimed below.

WHAT CAN BE ANSWERED IS THE HALF THAT IS ARITHMETIC. This branch's standing rule is that A COST IS
A FRACTION OF RISK, NOT A NUMBER OF POINTS -- the failure recorded in STUDY_TURTLE_15M was charging
NQ's 1.72-point round turn in GOLD's points and reporting PF 0.35 as a decisive failure, when the
same cost was 54.2% of gold's stop against 3.7% of NQ's. The IB-25 rule sizes its stop as a
FRACTION OF THE MORNING RANGE, and a morning range is roughly a constant PERCENTAGE of the index
whatever the index is. So the ES question that does not need ES bars is: at ES's tick and point
value, what fraction of that risk does a round turn consume, and does the NQ verdict move when the
NQ series is charged that fraction instead of its own?

That is run below. It isolates ES's COST STRUCTURE from ES's PRICE PATH and answers the first
exactly, while leaving the second untestable and saying so.


==========================================================================================================================
STEP 1 -- WHAT THE RULE'S RISK ACTUALLY IS, measured on the one market that can run it
==========================================================================================================================
  527 trades. Risk = (stop_frac - retr) x morning range = 0.25 x range.
  risk in POINTS   median   29.19   p25  22.50   p75  39.69
  risk as % of ENTRY PRICE   median 0.1509%   p25 0.1129%   p75 0.2013%
  morning range as % of price  median 0.6086%

  A morning range is a percentage of the index, not a number of points, so this % risk is
  the quantity that carries across markets. ES's own IB range runs a comparable fraction of
  price -- both are US equity indices trading the same 09:30 auction -- while its TICK is a
  much larger fraction of its price. That is the whole of the ES cost question.

==========================================================================================================================
STEP 2 -- THE ROUND TURN AS A FRACTION OF THAT RISK, per contract
==========================================================================================================================
  Assuming the same 0.1509% risk on both indices (1 tick a side of slippage,
  entry on a limit charged conservatively as if it paid the spread too).

  contract                  pt val   tick  fee RT $  slip RT   RT pts  RT % of px  risk pts  RT as % of RISK
  MNQ  Micro Nasdaq-100          2   0.25      1.44     0.50    1.220     0.0049%     37.72             3.2%
  MES  Micro S&P 500             5   0.25      1.44     0.50    0.788     0.0116%     10.26             7.7%
  NQ   E-mini Nasdaq-100        20   0.25      4.28     0.50    0.714     0.0029%     37.72             1.9%
  ES   E-mini S&P 500           50   0.25      4.28     0.50    0.586     0.0086%     10.26             5.7%

  MES pays 2.37x the fraction of risk that MNQ pays.
  The dollar cost is lower and the RELATIVE cost is higher, because ES's 0.25 tick is
  0.0037% of its price against 0.0010% for NQ -- 3.7x -- and the
  fee is spread over a 2.5x smaller point count. Cheaper in dollars is not cheaper in R.

==========================================================================================================================
STEP 4 -- THE ZERO-COST VARIANT. Is ES's cost the objection, or is the rule the objection?
==========================================================================================================================
  GROSS, no fee and no slippage at all   research  n  345   -0.00466 %/trade   PF 0.930   win 48.4%
  GROSS, no fee and no slippage at all   locked    n  182   +0.01942 %/trade   PF 1.235   win 57.1%

  This is the number that decides it. A cost problem is a rule that makes money gross and
  loses it net -- better fills, a cheaper broker or a bigger contract would then rescue it.

==========================================================================================================================
STEP 5 -- THE WIN RATE AGAINST ITS OWN DRIFTLESS BREAK-EVEN, which is a property of the
==========================================================================================================================
  GEOMETRY and not of the market. Target = the range extreme, stop = stop_frac of the range.
  reward:risk = (retr - 0) : (stop_frac - retr), so the driftless bound is 1/(1+RR).

    retr   stop      RR  break-even  actual win  net %/trade  gross %/tr
    0.25   0.50   1.000       50.0%       51.4%     -0.00767    +0.00366
    0.25   0.75   0.500       66.7%       68.1%     -0.00447    +0.00686
    0.35   0.50   2.333       30.0%       26.7%     -0.02115    -0.00988
    0.50   0.75   2.000       33.3%       34.9%     -0.00685    +0.00447
    0.50   1.00   1.000       50.0%       51.1%     +0.00893    +0.02025

  The win rate tracks the break-even the geometry implies at every rung. Moving the stop to
  75% does raise the win rate exactly as the post claims -- and it raises the break-even by
  the same amount, which is why expectancy does not follow.

==========================================================================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
==========================================================================================================================
  ESTABLISHED, and it is arithmetic rather than a backtest: at equal percentage risk an MES
  round turn is 2.37x the fraction of risk an MNQ round turn is. ES is the cheaper
  contract in dollars and the more expensive one in R, so the cost objection to this rule is
  LARGER on ES than on the market it was measured on, not smaller.

  NOT ESTABLISHED, and not testable here: whether the S&P's 09:30-10:30 auction retraces
  differently from the Nasdaq's. That needs ES 1-minute bars. The strategy below is shipped
  so it can be measured on them.
